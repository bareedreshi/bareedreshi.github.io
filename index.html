Agency contract review and renegotiation

Situation

An SEO agency engagement arrived already in motion: a large one-time "local presence rollout" plus a monthly hours block, with access granted and an invoice issued.

Found

The rollout was a citation build priced several times above what the standard tooling costs for the same output. The monthly block was sold as capacity rather than deliverables, with contract language stating outright that no requested item was guaranteed. Scope overlapped the paid media accounts I had just rebuilt in house. Terms ran one direction: full payment upfront and non-refundable, low liability caps, out-of-state venue, unilateral rate increases.

Action

Recommended killing the rollout, carving paid media out of scope entirely, restructuring the monthly block around defined deliverables, and fixing the legal terms before any renewal. Framed upward as financial stewardship rather than territorial defence.

The tell

Their flagship deliverable targeted directory listings. Our actual problem was Google Business Profile completeness. Right invoice, wrong lever, which is the thing you only catch if you already know where the visibility is leaking.

Vendor access governance

Situation

Two vendors working the same domain at the same time: an SEO agency running off-site local work, and a development partner mid-build on a platform migration.

Practice

Least privilege by default. Analytics, Search Console, Ads, Meta, TikTok and Shopify granted at editor rather than admin, with admin downgraded once onboarding was done. Merchant Center and developer seats held back until scope was settled. Registrar and DNS control never handed over. When bot mitigation was needed, the answer was a scoped CDN member seat or an in-house implementation, not the keys to the domain.

Why

A vendor holding DNS during an active migration is a single point of failure for the whole business. Coordination costs a few emails. A rollback costs the quarter.

AI-augmented marketing operations

Built

A named agent system rather than a pile of scripts: spend-anomaly detection, review management across the location footprint, recurring reporting, and storefront and site health monitoring. Roughly ten workflows on n8n and the Claude API, with model routing by task and approval gates on anything that touches spend.

Design call

Built the revenue side webhook-driven off commerce events rather than polling the ads API for conversions. Conversion reporting carries hours of processing latency, so polling it produces alerts on data that has not settled. Platform-reported metrics stay separated from commerce-actual revenue in every output, and Performance Max asset-group data is treated as modeled: useful for creative direction, never for unit economics.

Validation

Cross-model review, where a second model critiques the first's output before anything ships. Adversarial QA beats a single model agreeing with itself.

Result

Replaced a full day of manual weekly reporting and redirected that capacity into strategy and testing.

Note

Built without engineering support. The Computer Science background is what makes that possible. I ship the system rather than write a requirements doc and wait for someone else to.

Graduate research

REI: when membership growth stops paying for itself

Question

Does REI's membership growth actually translate into profitability? Built a three-dataset view of the co-op: four years of high-level financials, store-level inventory and sales, and a customer service and returns log, strategic, operational and customer perspectives on one business.

Found

Membership grew from roughly 21 million to 25 million between 2021 and 2024, close to 20% growth. Over the same window the co-op went from a $97.7 million profit to three consecutive loss years, with net sales falling from $3.85 billion to $3.53 billion. Correlation between member growth and net income came out around negative 0.59.

Conclusion

Acquisition was working and unit economics were not. New members were not spending enough to cover the cost of acquiring and serving them. A community-building success in direct conflict with financial sustainability.

Why it stuck

It is the counterweight to a career spent optimizing for more customers. Growth that outruns contribution margin is a slow way to lose money, and it is the reason I value conversions rather than counting them.

Client Campaign Analytics System: database design

Built

A full relational design for a marketing department running campaigns on behalf of clients: conceptual model, logical schema and physical design with SQL. Entities for clients, campaigns, channels (Google Ads, Meta, LinkedIn, email), audience segments defined by demographic and behavioral criteria, performance records carrying impressions, clicks, conversions and spend by reporting period, and team members.

The interesting part

Two many-to-many relationships needed resolving. A campaign targets multiple audience segments and a segment gets reused across campaigns. A campaign has multiple people on it and a person works multiple campaigns. Both resolved with junction tables, so targeting definitions stay consistent instead of being duplicated per campaign.

Why it matters

This is the data model underneath every reporting layer I have built since. Knowing why the schema looks the way it does is the difference between reading a dashboard and being able to fix one.
